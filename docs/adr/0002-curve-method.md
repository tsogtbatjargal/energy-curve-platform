# 2. Curve method: spot anchor plus seasonal historical shape, Spot and C1–C4

Date: 2026-10-02 · Revised 2026-10-03 · Status: accepted

## Context
EIA publishes current daily spot prices for WTI and Brent. It stopped publishing NYMEX futures on 2024-04-05, and its futures history covers WTI contracts 1–4 only. "Contract 1" is the nearest contract to expire: a rolling position, not a fixed delivery month. EIA has no Brent futures.

## Decision

**Labels.** Points are `Spot, C1, C2, C3, C4`. These are contract positions, not months ahead and not delivery months; mapping positions to delivery months would need the NYMEX expiry calendar and is out of scope.

**Shape estimation** (`curves/shape.py`, Polars; the M5 PySpark job must reproduce it):
- `s[k, m]` = median of `ln(Ck / Spot)` over every date in calendar month `m` within the window 2014-01-01 to 2024-04-05, using WTI spot (RWTC) and RCLC1–RCLC4 on the same date.
- `ln` and the median are float64. Each `s[k, m]` is quantized to 10 decimal places, round half even, from the float's shortest round-trip representation.
- Every position/month pair needs at least 15 observations, or estimation fails.
- Non-positive prices stay in Silver and Gold. They are excluded from estimation only, because `ln` is undefined for them. The excluded rows are written out with reasons; in the real data the only excluded date is 2020-04-20.

**Reproducibility.** Results are compared as sorted data, not file bytes:
- keys and `n_obs` must match exactly
- `|s_a − s_b| ≤ 1e-9`, one unit in the 10th decimal place, which covers a value that rounds the other way at the boundary

That comparison applies to:
- the stored parameters checked against a fresh estimate (`verify-shape`)
- the Polars implementation checked against PySpark (M5)

**Where parameters live (ADR-0011):**
- **Real-derived parameters stay local.** They are written to the git-ignored `data/shape/`, tagged `origin: "eia"`. `data_manifests/shape_inputs.eia.json` records only row counts and SHA-256 hashes, which the opt-in `realdata` tests check.
- **Public parameters are synthetic.** `src/energy_curves/curves/params/synthetic-shape-v1.{csv,json}` is generated by `energy-curves write-synthetic-params` from the synthetic source only. A test regenerates it and requires the same input hash, the same parameter hash, and a clean comparison.
- **Origin must match.** A run refuses parameters whose `origin` differs from the data it ingests, and shape estimation refuses inputs that mix sources.

**Outright curve** for spot `S` on as-of date `d`:
- `Spot = S`
- `Ck = S × exp(s[k, month(d)])`

**Arithmetic:**
- `Decimal` in a 34-significant-digit context (IEEE 754 decimal128).
- `Decimal.exp` is correctly rounded, so prices do not depend on platform floating point.
- Prices are quantized to 4 decimal places, round half even.

**Brent** borrows WTI's `s[k, m]`, and its `shape_source` says so.

**Brent − WTI spread** at each position is the displayed Brent minus the displayed WTI, computed from the already-rounded outright prices. The spread shown therefore always equals the difference of the curves shown. Because the shapes are shared, it equals `(B − W) × exp(s[k, m])` before rounding.

**Dates.** Curves are built only for dates with an anchor spot. A spread needs both spots on the same date; otherwise every position is a gap with a reason. Nothing is forward-filled. A non-positive anchor is also a gap: a multiplicative shape applied to a negative price is meaningless.

**Labelling.** Every curve point carries `estimate_type = "modelled"`, `shape_source`, `method_version`, `shape_method_version` and `params_sha256`. CSV exports start with `# Modelled estimate, not market quotes`.

## Consequences
- The method is simple to explain and test, and its parameters can be inspected and reproduced.
- The curves are a model, not market quotes, and every output says so.
- Because Brent borrows WTI's shape, the Brent − WTI spread changes little across positions.
- Delivery-month labels, tenors beyond C4 and stochastic models are left as later exercises.
